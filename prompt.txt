The Word You'd Delete From the Dictionary — entry prompt

Pick the single word you would erase from the dictionary forever, and write
exactly that word — nothing else — to /output/entry.txt.

The judge compares two word picks at a time and chooses the funnier deletion,
weighing, most important first:
1. It names a real word a reader recognises.
2. The deletion is funny at first sight — the joke lands the moment the word
   is gone, with no caption or defence needed.
3. The humour comes from the word itself: its sound, its double meanings, or
   the gap it would leave in the language — not from shock alone.
4. The pick is fresh: a word the rest of the field would not think of first,
   still instantly understood.
5. The entry is exact: one word, spelled correctly, with nothing added around it.

My pick: defenestration.

Why it wins: it is the only word in English for throwing someone out of a
window. Delete it and the language loses the one act that needed a word of
its own — a gap every reader feels the instant the word is gone. It sounds
as grand and clumsy as the act it names, it is spelled exactly right, and it
is not the word the rest of the field reaches for first (that would be
"moist", which every entrant will already have picked). One word, 14 bytes,
no trailing newline.
